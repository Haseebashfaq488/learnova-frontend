Calculating Net Force and Acceleration in 2D Systems

Master orthogonal vector resolution, tilted frame transformation, and downhill dynamic equilibrium for inclined physics problems.

01

Orthogonal Vector Resolution on Inclines

When analyzing rigid bodies accelerating down an inclined surface, computing force vectors along traditional horizontal (x) and vertical (y) axes causes simultaneous acceleration along both dimensions. To simplify Newton's second law calculations, we perform a coordinate rotation: we orient the chosen x'-axis strictly parallel to the surface incline, and the y'-axis orthogonal (perpendicular) to the contact plane.

Under this rotated reference system, perpendicular acceleration collapses to zero (ay' = 0), meaning all net accelerating dynamics isolate cleanly into the parallel axis (ax').

functions Governing Law • 2D Component Equations SI Units: [N] • [kg·m/s²]
Parallel Incline Axis Σ Fx = m · ax Fg,∥ − fk = m · ax
Perpendicular Axis (Equilibrium) Σ Fy = m · ay = 0 FN − Fg,⊥ = 0

Gravity vector Fg = m · g decomposes directly into orthogonal components using the ramp angle θ.

02

Strategic Coordinate Rotation Steps

  • explore
    Align Coordinate System with Ramp Orient the reference frame such that +x points directly down the slope and +y points perpendicular outward. This guarantees zero normal acceleration (ay = 0).
  • call_split
    Decompose Gravitational Force Vectors Decompose downward gravity into parallel and orthogonal components relative to the incline surface:
    Fg,∥ = m · g · sin(θ) Fg,⊥ = m · g · cos(θ)
  • balance
    Solve for Normal Force and Kinetic Friction Equilibrium across the perpendicular axis yields FN = Fg,⊥ = m · g · cos(θ). Consequently, kinetic friction simplifies directly to fk = μk · FN = μk · m · g · cos(θ).

How do I decide whether to use sine or cosine for the gravity component along an incline?

10:42 AM
neurology
Learnova Tutor

Great question! Remember this simple rule of thumb:

  1. 1. When ramp angle θ = 0° (flat ground), gravity points directly into the surface, so normal force equals full gravity: FN = mg · cos(0°) = mg
  2. 2. As the ramp gets steeper (θ → 90°), the sliding force down the ramp increases: Fparallel = mg · sin(θ)

• Use sin(θ) for the downhill parallel force.
• Use cos(θ) for the perpendicular into-the-ramp force.
